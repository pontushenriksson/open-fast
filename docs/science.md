# Science and sources

Every figure in Open Fast was checked against the original publication in October 2026. This page lists the sources and how they are used. If you find an error, please [open an issue](https://github.com/pontushenriksson/open-fast/issues) with a link to the evidence.

## What the research says, in short

- **Weight:** with the same number of calories, intermittent fasting gives about the same weight loss as ordinary calorie restriction. Its advantage is that some people find it easier to stick to.
- **Timing may matter:** early eating windows have improved blood pressure and insulin sensitivity in small trials, even without weight loss.
- **Long-term data is thin:** most trials last 8–12 weeks. Observational data on very short eating windows is debated.
- **Autophagy:** well documented in animals and cells; the timing in humans is unknown.

## "Does it break my fast?"

Verdicts follow a simple, transparent rule set:

- **Fine:** no meaningful energy and no insulin response (water, black coffee, tea, electrolytes).
- **Gray zone:** a few calories or an uncertain/individual response (a splash of milk, diet soda, gum).
- **Breaks:** meaningful energy, sugar or protein (which signals "fed" via insulin and mTOR).

Calorie values are typical figures for the stated amount and vary between products. Autophagy impact is marked as theoretical because human data is lacking.
